# ERSA Intermediate DSGE Modelling and Policy Applications
## Participant overview and preparation

**9–13 November 2026 | University of Cape Town**  

We will move from economic intuition to implementation, model extensions and interpretation. The emphasis is on understanding how a modelling choice produces a result and what that result means.

The [detailed schedule](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1ZY6m46xt7Z1jF3WgcaSP3GxivPZ8YxHT/edit) is also linked through the [workshop repository’s Schedule section](https://github.com/hollander03/dsge-training/tree/main/training#schedule). Monday–Thursday run from 09:00 to 17:00. Friday runs from 09:00 to 15:00.

## Before the workshop

1. Complete the **thesis logic document** before the workshop starts. Set out the project you want to work on: your core question, why the chosen model/method can answer it, the mechanism or comparison you will examine, and the result you intend to investigate. It may concern an existing thesis, a new project or a focused exercise using an established model. This gives us a starting point for feedback and a record of your plans. Submission arrangements will accompany the document.
2. Complete the **[pre-workshop Day 1 survey](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/636QCVY)**, also linked at the top of the repository.
3. Bring your laptop for practical sessions and follow the repository’s software/setup guidance. Bring any model code and relevant documentation you already use. If you plan to use an existing model, identify it and its associated paper/documentation in advance where possible.

You may work with your own model or an existing model from the Macroeconomic Model Data Base (MMB). Locally saved MMB models will be available, so access to the website is not required for the workshop. Choose a manageable question that can be explored during the week.

## Monday–Wednesday: the daily format

| Time | Activity | What to expect |
|---|---|---|

| 10:30–11:00 | Tea | |
| 11:00–12:30 | Minimal example and guided practical | The facilitator demonstrates a small code example and explains its results. We connect the concepts to implementation. |
| 12:30–13:30 | Lunch | |
| 13:30–15:00 | Group extension | Work in your group to extend the example. Suggested exercises will be supplied, with scope to pursue a related direction of your choice. |
| 15:00–15:30 | Tea | |
| 15:30–17:00 | Preparation and group presentations | First 30 minutes: preparation. Each of the five groups then has **8 minutes to present and 2 minutes for questions**. |

There are five groups of up to four participants, each with a group lead. Use the group to discuss mechanisms, share implementation tasks and troubleshoot. The facilitator will be available throughout.

For Monday–Wednesday presentations, use **no more than four content slides, excluding the title slide**. Show what you tried, the method or change you made, a result and its interpretation. **Every group member other than the lead must present at least once across these three days.** Agree the rotation within your group. Leads can help prepare and discuss the work while other members take the presentation role.

The broad progression is growth models on Monday, RBC models on Tuesday, and New Keynesian models and monetary-policy applications on Wednesday. Consult the detailed schedule for the specific examples.

## Thursday: apply the methods to your project

The morning covers welfare analysis and optimal policy, followed by practical work on Bayesian estimation and identification. The first session remains pencil and paper only.

**13:30–15:00:** apply relevant methods to your own project or an identified existing model. This work should connect to the thesis logic document you completed before the workshop. A focused calibration, diagnostic, policy-rule comparison, sensitivity exercise or counterfactual is enough for a useful workshop application.

**15:30–17:00:** prepare your individual presentation for Friday. You can use your group for feedback and troubleshooting, with the facilitator available to help.

## Friday: individual presentations and discussion

The 13 participants other than the group leads will present individually. Each has **10 minutes to present, followed by 5 minutes of questions and discussion**. Use **a maximum of eight slides including the title slide**. Group leads may present afterwards if time permits.

Build the presentation around **one core question, a focused method and a result**:

- Introduce your title and give only the brief context needed to understand the question.
- Describe the model generally, then explain the particular mechanism, equation, parameter, policy rule or identification choice needed to understand your result.
- Show the result clearly and interpret the economic mechanism. State the shock/comparison, relevant units and assumptions, and any important limitation.
- Close with the main lesson and the next step or question on which you would welcome feedback.

A literature review, a contribution section and a complete description of the model are unnecessary for this presentation. The focus is **methods and interpreting results**. If implementation remains incomplete, explain the precise diagnostic or obstacle, what you tested and what you learned; do not present anticipated results as findings.

Presentations will be organised across 09:00–10:30, 11:00–12:30 and 13:30–15:00. Please have your slides ready in advance so changeovers are brief. The workshop closes at 15:00.

We look forward to working through your questions and models together.
