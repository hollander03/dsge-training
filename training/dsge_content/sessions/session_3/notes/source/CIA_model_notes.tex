\section*{CIA: model notes}
Based on \texttt{CIA.mod}; Walsh (2010), Appendix 3.6, pp. 128--130.

\subsection*{Underlying preferences}
\[
U(c,1-n)=\frac{c^{1-\eta_c}}{1-\eta_c}
 +\Psi\frac{(1-n)^{1-\eta_n}}{1-\eta_n}.
\]
At $\eta_n=1$, the leisure term is $\Psi\log(1-n)$.
The binding cash-in-advance constraint implies $c_t=m_t$ in equilibrium.
The variable $\lambda$ (\texttt{lambdda}) is the marginal value of wealth,
not marginal utility of consumption: the cash constraint creates a wedge.

\subsection*{Scope and calibration}
These are stationary, log-linear business-cycle equations. Quantities are
log deviations; $r$, $i$ and $\pi$ are level deviations of net rates.
The system uses small-rate approximations, not exact gross-rate scaling.
The leisure curvature implies a Frisch elasticity
$(1-n^{ss})/(\eta_n n^{ss})$.

The code fixes \texttt{nss}; $\Psi$ is implicit. Changing trend inflation
while retaining \texttt{nss} changes the implied leisure weight. It does not
trace steady-state hours with preferences fixed. Additive leisure preferences
also generally do not support constant hours along balanced growth unless
consumption-composite curvature is one; these exercises abstract from growth.

\section*{Linear equations implemented in the code}
All equations are conditional on information at $t$; future variables in
Dynare imply conditional expectations. Hats are suppressed, as in the code.
Write $YK=y^{ss}/k^{ss}$, $CK=YK-\delta$ and $NL=n^{ss}/(1-n^{ss})$.
\begin{align*}
y_t&=z_t+\alpha k_{t-1}+(1-\alpha)n_t,\\
YK\,y_t&=CK\,c_t+\delta x_t,\\
k_t&=(1-\delta)k_{t-1}+\delta x_t,\\
r_t&=\alpha YK(E_ty_{t+1}-k_t),\\
c_t&=m_t,\\
\lambda_t&=E_t\lambda_{t+1}+r_t,\\
y_t+\lambda_t&=(1+\eta_n NL)n_t,\\
i_t&=r_t+E_t\pi_{t+1},\\
\lambda_t&=-E_t(\eta_c m_{t+1}+\pi_{t+1}),\\
m_t&=m_{t-1}-\pi_t+\mu_t,\\
z_t&=\rho_z z_{t-1}+\epsilon_{z,t},\\
\mu_t&=\rho_\mu\mu_{t-1}+\phi_z z_{t-1}+\epsilon_{\mu,t}.
\end{align*}
In code: $\alpha=\texttt{alppha}$, $\beta=\texttt{betta}$,
$i=\texttt{int}$, $\pi=\texttt{pii}$, investment is \texttt{x}.
The current calibration sets $\phi_z=0$.
